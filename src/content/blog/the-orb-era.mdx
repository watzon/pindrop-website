The blog went quiet after v1.11. The app did the opposite: eleven releases
between March and this week, including the two biggest updates Pindrop has
ever shipped. If you only read one catch-up post, make it this one. If you
want the play-by-play instead, this site now has a proper
[changelog](/changelog) that gets updated with every release.

## The app got a new face

Version 1.19 introduced the **Orb**: a living, audio-reactive indicator that
breathes while idle, swells when you speak, and streams your words next to it
as you dictate. It replaced the old pill and dot indicators as the default,
and it has become the face of the app. (If you preferred the quieter styles,
1.21 brought back a discreet notch pill and a bubble that follows your caret.)

Then 1.20 rebuilt everything around it. The release went by the codename
*Scorched Earth* internally, and it earned it: a focused sidebar, a new Home
dashboard, a rebuilt Library, standalone Settings, and polished onboarding.
Updates now greet you with an in-app What's New sheet, so you can see what
changed without hunting through release notes.

## Words appear as you say them

Transcription used to be something that happened after you stopped talking.
Three engine additions changed that:

- **Nemotron streaming** (1.19) shows words the moment you say them, with
  tentative text that settles into place as recognition commits. The engine
  prewarms at launch, so your first dictation starts without a spin-up delay.
- **SenseVoice** (1.21) brings fast, fully local multilingual recognition
  via FunASR's SenseVoice-Small model.
- **Apple's on-device speech** (1.18) is now built in as a zero-download
  option using the macOS system recognizer.

Together with WhisperKit and the Parakeet models that have been on board
since the 1.x early days, that makes five local engines. Pick accuracy,
latency, language coverage, or zero setup per task. Long dictations also got
optional on-device paragraph formatting in 1.21, which breaks walls of
spoken text into readable paragraphs.

## Pindrop learned some languages

In March, Pindrop spoke English. Today the interface ships in more than
thirty languages, with full right-to-left layout support, and dictation
gained Russian and Ukrainian (thanks to [@nezzard](https://github.com/nezzard)),
plus Polish, Hindi, and Malayalam in 1.21.

Contributor work landed in the audio stack too:
[@ntdkhang](https://github.com/ntdkhang) helped track down why Bluetooth
headphones as output would break external microphone input. Since 1.19,
your mic no longer follows your output route.

## A Library that earns the name

The rebuilt Library from 1.20 is where dictations actually live now: browse,
search, sort, replay, edit, export. Around it grew a set of features for
people who dictate a lot:

- **Speaker profiles**: for meeting and imported-media transcripts, train a
  voice once and diarization labels who said what, with offline global
  clustering keeping labels consistent.
- **Notes and dictionary**: spoken notes with interactive checklists, and a
  custom vocabulary with match modes and usage-aware transcription bias.
- **Activity insights**: streaks, word counts, destinations, and yearly
  trends on the Home view.
- **Audio retention**: keep recordings at native quality with configurable
  cleanup, waveform previews, and per-recording export.
- **Editable transcripts and pipeline insights** (1.22): fix a saved
  transcript in place, and inspect stage-by-stage timing for any dictation.

## Privacy you can check

1.22 added optional diagnostics through TelemetryDeck, and the design
matters more than the feature: it is **off until you opt in**, it reports
bucketed timing and token metrics rather than transcript content, and the
separate training-contribution workflow is local-only. You can review,
export, or delete every redacted transcript pair on your machine, and
uploading is disabled in the app, full stop.

None of this asks for trust. The code is MIT-licensed and
[on GitHub](https://github.com/watzon/pindrop), so the claims are checkable.

## Odds and ends worth knowing

- An embedded **MCP server** (1.18) lets AI tools drive Pindrop in agent
  workflows.
- Update windows now show **release notes in-app** before you install.
- Text insertion works reliably **inside virtual machines** (1.21), and a
  dedicated **cancel hotkey** stops a recording mid-flight.
- Pindrop can **launch silently** in the menu bar without opening the main
  window.
